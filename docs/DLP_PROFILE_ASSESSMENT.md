# Are the thesis's DLP fragments still useful?

Assessment date: 9 September 2026. This separates the historical language, this reconstruction's supported input, and the conditions required by individual optimizations. It is an assessment of scope and evidence, not a claim that a new language fragment or reasoning algorithm has been discovered.

**Yes: DLP remains useful as a semantic foundation, a subject for specialized reasoning research, and an internal capability decomposition. Its theoretical or computational value does not depend on measuring how frequently suitable ontologies occur.** The present L0–L3 labels should still be distinguished from standard OWL profile names in interoperability claims. OWL 2 RL explicitly identifies Description Logic Programs as an influence. This is evidence of the original approach's lasting relevance, rather than evidence that RL made its underlying ideas irrelevant. [W3C OWL 2 Profiles, §4](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/#OWL_2_RL)

In particular, a correct L3 reasoner that demonstrably executes a matched reasoning task faster than an implementation supporting a more expressive language establishes a useful specialization for those inputs and that task. This conclusion does not require an estimate of their prevalence. Whether to prioritize its deployment across a portfolio of applications is a different question.

The completed finite existential-bridge comparison now provides that scoped computational evidence: the preserved `b1254c4` baseline and frozen candidate both answered the same consistency and named-instance task faster than the pinned HermiT distribution. All 27 timed workers matched independently justified exact answers. This is an implementation result on a controlled family, not a new complexity theorem or a universal ranking of reasoners. [Results and protocol](hermit-comparator-plan.md)

## Three different classifications

| Classification | What it describes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis L0–L3 | Constructor positions and their DL/FOL translations: a Horn core; equality; denials; then existential consequents. | Membership in a later W3C OWL 2 profile or a particular implementation's completeness. |
| This repository's `L0`–`L3` options | Accepted RDF encodings and compiler capabilities, including documented normalizations and datatype limits. | A complete OWL syntax/profile conformance check. |
| Evaluator eligibility | Properties of the compiled rules and current state: function freedom, equality, join shape, complete old closure, and update type. | A new ontology language, or a performance guarantee from a profile label. |

The source for the first row is Volz's *Web Ontology Reasoning with Logic Databases* (2004), chapter 5, especially printed pp. 115–123. The source for the second is the actual compiler and [supported-semantics policy](../README.md). The source for the third is the implementation and its recorded experiments. See the [thesis specification](THESIS_SPEC.md), [errata](THESIS_ERRATA.md), and [official thesis record](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/162004).

The historical layers provide a useful explanation of where equality, inconsistency checks, and generated witnesses enter the computation. They are less useful as the only classifier of execution cost. An L0 ontology can already contain expensive binary transitive joins; a program accepted under L2 can contain only simple unary implications. Enabling a higher compiler ceiling does not add absent axioms to the program.

## The relationship to OWL 2 RL is an overlap, not automatic inclusion

RL's superclass grammar excludes general existential restrictions, so `A ⊑ ∃R.B` belongs to the thesis's L3 extension but is outside syntactic RL. A has-value consequent `A ⊑ ∃R.{a}` is different: it names its filler and is permitted. Even L1's `⊤ ⊑ A` is outside RL's subclass grammar, which excludes `owl:Thing` there. These are syntax-membership statements, not proofs that no useful equivalent rewriting exists. RL also admits property chains and keys, subject to its restrictions; this compiler rejects both. [W3C OWL 2 Profiles, §§4.2.3–4.2.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/#Profile_Specification_3)

Consequently, the precise reusable target is an explicitly checked intersection of a thesis fragment and an OWL profile, together with a stated datatype and query contract. Calling all L0–L2 inputs “OWL RL subsets” without these qualifications is too strong. Calling L3 “more expressive than OWL RL” also needs care: L3 adds a direction of existential reasoning, while this implementation lacks other RL features.

Global restrictions are a second independent obstacle. OWL 2 DL requires simple properties in functionality and cardinality axioms; a transitive property is not simple. For example, declaring the same `:p` both transitive and functional is accepted by this compiler's characteristic-to-rule translation, but fails the OWL 2 DL global restrictions, and hence is not an OWL 2 RL ontology. The rules still have an understandable FOL interpretation. Parser acceptance therefore cannot certify OWL 2 DL/RL membership. Property-hierarchy regularity and entity typing also need validation when claiming standard conformance. [W3C Structural Specification, §§5.8 and 11](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-syntax/#Global_Restrictions_on_Axioms_in_OWL_2_DL)

Datatype support is a separate implementation gap. The repository stores RDF literals, recognizes selected string, decimal/integer and Boolean value identities, and rejects explicit datatype constraints. That is not full RL datatype reasoning. RDF parsing, a successful run, or correct answers on datatype-free tests cannot establish datatype completeness. The thesis itself excluded datatype properties and restrictions from its benchmark scope (printed p. 195); this historical choice does not excuse a modern conformance claim.

## “OWL RL reasoning” can refer to different contracts

OWL 2 RL/RDF rules operate over triples and can be applied outside syntactic RL; their application does not turn arbitrary input into a conforming RL ontology. Their relationship to Direct Semantics is qualified by Theorem PR1. [W3C OWL 2 Profiles, §4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/#Reasoning_in_OWL_2_RL_and_RDF_Graphs_using_Rules)

In particular, that theorem requires no IRI to play multiple entity roles, excludes certain annotation-property axioms in the premise ontology, and restricts the conclusion to listed atomic assertions involving named individuals. Conformance distinguishes a proven negative answer from an unresolved entailment outside the completeness conditions. These distinctions also matter for query-answer comparisons. [W3C Conformance, §§2.2.1–2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-conformance/#Entailment_Checker)

This repository uses class/property predicates and equality on individual arguments. The associated Direct-Semantics-style distinction is material: equality between two individuals does not in itself identify classes or properties that happen to use their names. A triple reasoner that rewrites those positions may be computing additional RDF-Based consequences. A benchmark should compare the intended named assertions or query answers over the shared semantic fragment, not require two differently specified engines to serialize identical entire RDF closures. [W3C Direct Semantics, §§2.1–2.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-direct-semantics/)

## Where the narrower fragments still help

1. **Predictable finite materialization.** Function-free rules permit finite active-domain closure. The L3 Skolem extension can generate an unbounded chain; a resource bound gives an explicitly incomplete result, not a completeness theorem. This separation remains operationally useful. The thesis already records the termination boundary in §5.5.2 and excludes nonconstant functions from chapter 6 maintenance (p. 165, footnote 5).
2. **Targeted maintenance and query plans.** Equality-free, function-free programs can use the current DRed path without rebuilding equality classes or witnesses. A same-variable unary conjunction admits the repository's specialized set-based evaluator. These conditions are narrower than DLP as a whole and cut across standard profiles. They should be detected on compiled rules rather than inferred from an `L0`–`L3` label alone.
3. **Small, auditable semantics.** A constrained compiler is valuable for reproducible research, regression tests, and applications whose schemas demonstrably fit it. Correctness depends on accurately rejecting unsupported axioms and testing every claimed inference obligation; theoretical interest does not require widespread deployment.
4. **Historical and experimental controls.** The layers isolate costs associated with equality and witnesses when reproducing the original study. They also provide disciplined test families for optimizations. This is a valid scientific use even if a production interface eventually exposes standard profiles.

Deployment has additional costs: ontology conversion, rejected standard features, a smaller tool ecosystem, conformance validation, and a narrower completeness contract. Those costs affect adoption decisions, not whether a fragment has mathematical interest or permits useful specialization. Current gains in unary evaluation or support certificates are evidence about particular compiled workloads. They do not show that requiring users to adopt DLP syntax is faster than accepting the same ontology through an RL frontend. The adaptive and certificate experiments also contain neutral results and regressions; they do not justify a universal speed claim. See [research experiments](RESEARCH_EXPERIMENTS.md).

## What the current LUBM result establishes

The [official LUBM(1,0) evaluation](../benchmarks/lubm-results.md) loaded the full official ontology under L3, retained generated witnesses, and matched all 14 official named-answer sets in all nine workers. Existential consequents prevent that unchanged ontology from compiling under this repository's lower profiles.

That proves neither that witnesses are essential to those 14 answer sets nor that all unqueried consequences are correct. A complete ontology and a query-sufficient fragment are different objects. An ablation could find that these specific queries need no generated witness, without establishing ontology equivalence. Conversely, existential rules can affect named answers: from `A(a)`, `A ⊑ ∃R.B`, and `∃R.B ⊑ C`, we obtain `C(a)`. Dropping the witness-generating inclusion without a justified rewriting loses that answer. This example follows directly from existential and subclass semantics. [W3C Direct Semantics, Tables 4–5](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-direct-semantics/#Class_Expressions)

The appropriate next test is to document any proposed query-preserving module or rewriting, compare all answer sets on multiple seeds and scales, and include cases whose answers genuinely depend on existential consequences. Passing the 14 queries alone cannot prove query preservation for every ABox or every query.

## Completed L3 task comparison against HermiT

The original controlled family uses the identical RDF/XML TBox `A ⊑ ∃R.B`, `∃R.B ⊑ C`, with `N` named A instances, one B-only named control and an unrelated class D. Every worker checks consistency, retrieves all named C instances and retrieves all named D instances. The independently expected answers are exactly the N A names for C and no names for D. A model with one shared B witness, the B-only control outside C and D empty establishes consistency and the negative answers. Removing either GCI permits C to be empty; all nine separate semantic smoke processes checked the full input and each omission across all three arms.

Both identifiers are retained in the evidence. This is not a claim about the latest upstream release. Eleven downloaded artifacts and all 43 resulting classpath jars are hash verified. Both existential GCIs are essential to C entailment, but explicit witness construction is not: their composition entails `A ⊑ C`. HermiT retains its normal optimization and realization behavior. The comparison concerns the same entailment task, not equality of entire closures or witness stores. The family also belongs to OWL 2 EL; these measurements do not establish superiority over specialized EL reasoners. Nor does the structural RL exclusion prevent an RDF-based subclass rule implementation from answering this particular C query. These limits preserve the demonstrated task advantage without converting it into a broader language or state-of-the-art claim. [W3C OWL 2 EL grammar](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/#OWL_2_EL)

## Theoretical merit, measured specialization, and deployment are different questions

Theoretical merit can be established by a precise language definition, translation correctness, decidability or complexity results for a specified reasoning problem, or a justified terminating algorithm on a stated subclass. None requires measuring ontology prevalence. A nonterminating unrestricted Skolem evaluation is an operational limitation; by itself, it does not prove that every decision procedure for the original fragment must fail to terminate. Conversely, observing finite evaluation on an acyclic benchmark does not prove termination on all L3 inputs.

For illustration, Hustadt, Motik and Sattler (IJCAI 2005, pp. 466–471) establish P-complete data complexity for specified Horn-SHIQ reasoning tasks, versus NP-complete satisfiability and coNP-complete instance checking for SHIQ under their ABox and query assumptions. This is prior theoretical evidence for specialization, not an identification of L3 with Horn-SHIQ or a complexity bound for our Skolem evaluator. [Author paper, §§3–4](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/boris.motik/pubs/hms05data.pdf)

Measured computational usefulness has a different but equally legitimate test: choose L3 inputs also handled by a reasoner for a more expressive language, require equivalent semantics and the same answers, and measure the same reasoning task. If the specialized system is reliably faster, that is positive evidence for its usefulness on that class of workloads, even if their real-world frequency is unknown. It establishes an implementation-level performance result, not a new asymptotic complexity theorem or universal superiority over all general-purpose reasoners. A broader engine can also incorporate special handling for the same fragment.

Such a comparison should use an explicitly identified common input domain, rather than assuming L3 includes RL or that every compiler-accepted L3 input meets OWL 2 DL's global restrictions. It need not involve OWL Full or a claim of complete general OWL Full reasoning. The task might be consistency, classification, named instance retrieval, or conjunctive-query answering; comparing different tasks or counting an incomplete witness prefix as a completed answer would not establish the proposed benefit.

For broader L3 evidence, include more ontologies and queries whose consequences depend on existential restrictions, both finite acyclic cases and recursive cases for which the evaluated methods give the required complete answers. Check those answers independently, measure witness-related costs where appropriate, and report resource exhaustion separately. The completed bridge experiment establishes a matched advantage on its finite family; extending that conclusion to recursive cases, other query forms or other general-purpose reasoners requires additional evidence. The LUBM result alone would not establish this comparison.

## Modern alternatives and a falsifiable deployment decision

OWL 2 EL is a natural candidate for large terminologies with existential class descriptions and classification needs. OWL 2 QL is aimed at query answering through relational database technology and query rewriting. RL suits rule-oriented reasoning. None of these three profiles contains either of the others; selecting one requires the workload and tooling contract. An EL-style classification method may avoid enumerating an infinite witness tree, but that does not automatically solve every conjunctive-query or L3 cardinality case. [W3C Primer, §§10.1–10.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-primer/#OWL_2_Profiles)

For this project, retain the historical labels for reproduction and further study of specialized reasoning, while making standard-conformance claims in terms of checked profiles and explicit extensions. Add supported RL features when they address real inputs; keep evaluator eligibility checks independent. Treat an L3 existential backend as a distinct research and implementation capability requiring termination and query-completeness evidence for its stated tasks.

A decision about maintaining an additional public frontend has further empirical criteria. They concern deployment priority, not prerequisites for the fragment's theoretical value:

- Measure coverage on representative published ontologies: fraction accepted unchanged, rejected constructors, and required semantic transformations.
- On identical inputs and answer contracts, compare end-to-end latency, peak memory, load cost, and fact/rule update costs against established engines. Include conversion and validation in a separate reported timing.
- Hold compiled rules fixed while enabling or disabling each specialization. This distinguishes an algorithm's benefit from an ontology restriction's cost.
- Test both favourable and adverse cases: sparse/dense joins, equality merges and splits, recursive unsupported cycles, and terminating/nonterminating existential patterns.
- Predeclare a material performance or deployment benefit and any application-specific coverage requirements. Reject the deployment tradeoff if the benefit disappears after equivalent semantics, conversion, memory and update costs are included; that outcome does not invalidate a separate theoretical result or a demonstrated benefit on another matched task.

The defensible present claim is that DLP supplied an enduring foundation and remains a worthwhile subject for specialized reasoning research. The completed bridge experiment strengthens that claim with an observed matched-task advantage against a pinned broader-language reasoner, without requiring any ontology-prevalence estimate. The result does not establish that the L0–L3 interface is a better modern interoperability target than OWL 2 RL or that the implementation outperforms established reasoners across other standard workloads.
