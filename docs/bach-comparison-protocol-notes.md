# Bach comparison: independent semantic and measurement audit

These notes audit the existing Bach query manifest and propose the contract for
a comparison of the current reasoner, the preserved `b1254c4` implementation,
HermiT, and ZodiacEdge. They are design notes, not benchmark observations. No
timings were collected for this audit.

## Source and scope

The two source examples must remain separate.

- **Complete ontology:** Table 2.5, printed p. 35, contains T1–T17 and A1–A15.
  A16 is an ellipsis, not an invitation to add historical facts. The supplied
  `examples/bach.ttl` and `examples/bach.owl` represent the same ontology.
- **Family maintenance:** Figure 6.2, p. 150; Table 6.1, p. 151; and Example
  6.3.2, pp. 158–159, supply a separate ancestor graph and two initial rules.
  The graph contains additional family relationships absent from Table 2.5.

Printed page numbers are 16 below one-based PDF page numbers. The source is
`docs/Volltext.pdf`; the text consulted for this audit is
`tmp/pdfs/thesis.txt`. These notes were checked against the source text, existing
OWL files, `examples/bach-queries.json`, `benchmarks/bach.py`, and
`tests/test_bach_benchmark.py`.

The three relevant editorial decisions must remain visible:

1. Table 2.5 T12 is parenthesized as a birthday cantata being a cantata and a
   homage, with a birthday event. Its displayed restriction has loose scope.
   Comparisons evaluate this documented reconstruction, not alternative
   unparenthesized readings.
2. Example 5.4.2, pp. 133–134, has ambiguous restriction scope. The existing
   intersection/union query explicitly places the person and gender conditions
   on the child. It is an adapted, disambiguated query.
3. On p. 159, the displayed `(jc,jc2)` insertion is read as `(js,jc2)`, following
   the preceding prose and resulting deltas. On p. 151, symmetry alone does not
   entail the stated cross-branch `inDynasty(jc1,c)` pair. Its negative expected
   answer is the corrected interpretation.

## Recommended comparison tracks

### Complete ontology and 25 questions

Run the current reasoner, the preserved baseline, and HermiT on the same RDF/XML
ontology bytes and the same 25 manifest questions. Preserve Turtle/RDF/XML graph
equivalence as a separate source validation. Report ZodiacEdge as outside the
contract for this track: the complete ontology requires existence statements,
constraints, and schema reasoning that a plain positive Datalog execution does
not supply. Feeding it consequences or named substitutes generated by another
reasoner would change the task and move inference into preparation.

source assertions:

Important reasons for the apparently sparse answers:

- A1 gives Johann Ambrosius an unnamed male child. A7, T2 and T5 make him a
  father. That child is not thereby identified with Johann Sebastian. Example
  4.5.2, p. 94, explicitly discusses this existential assertion.
- Wilhelm Friedemann is asserted to be a person, but is not classified as a man
  by the recorded ontology. Historical knowledge about his sex must not be
  injected into the query oracle. Consequently, Johann Sebastian is not an
  entailed answer to the two gender-qualified child queries.
- Married women do not become members of `Wife` from T3: the inclusion runs
  from `Wife` to its necessary conditions, not conversely. The same applies to
  `Husband`. Neither marriage nor shared surnames supplies motherhood.
- The two birthday events and required instruments need not have input names.
  Empty *named* answers do not imply that those things do not exist. Generated
  witnesses and their identifiers are outside cross-engine answer comparison.
- `owl:Thing` must remain included in the seven named types. Retrieve all
  entailed named types, not only the most specific ones.
- Negative results mean absence of entailment in a completed, consistent
  calculation, not entailed negation. The open-world explanation on p. 131
  does not license adding a closed-world maximum-one-child query.

The full Table 2.5 input has a terminating computation in the reconstruction.
This does not justify testing arbitrary fresh `Wife`/`Husband` instances by the
same procedure: their mutually recursive existence requirements can cause an
unending generated chain. Keep the existing `Father` satisfiability question;
do not silently extend it to every named class.

### Shared positive family task

All four engines can evaluate the family graph's positive binary rules. Compare
the complete named `ancestorOf` and `inDynasty` pair sets. The relation names
remain part of each output tuple: an ancestor pair and a dynasty pair are two
distinct logical statements.

The initial nine asserted ancestor edges are

```text
j→h, j→c, h→jc1, jc1→jm, jm→mb, mb→wf, js→wf, ja→js, c→ja
```

Names follow Figure 6.2: Johannes, Heinrich, Christoph, Johann Christoph,
Johann Michael, Maria Barbara, Wilhelm Friedemann, Johann Sebastian, and
Johann Ambrosius. They are recorded `ancestorOf` edges, not additional
`hasChild` facts. The initial rules express ancestor transitivity and ancestor
inclusion in dynasty. No dynasty symmetry is present until that update.

Each update begins from the original state, rather than following the previous
update. Expected outputs per original family copy are:

| State | Ancestor pairs | Dynasty pairs | Combined typed pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original | 24 | 24 | 48 |
| Remove `(js,wf)`, add `(js,jc2)` | 25 | 25 | 50 |
| Remove ancestor-to-dynasty rule | 24 | 0 | 24 |
| Add dynasty symmetry | 24 | 48 | 72 |

For the fact transaction, `jc2` denotes Johann Christian. The precise ancestor
loss is `{(js,wf),(ja,wf),(c,wf)}` and the precise gain is
`{(js,jc2),(ja,jc2),(c,jc2),(j,jc2)}`. Johannes remains an ancestor of Wilhelm
Friedemann through the other branch. The thesis presents the retraction as a
hypothetical maintenance example, not a historical finding about parentage.

After symmetry insertion, reverse all 24 ancestor pairs for dynasty, retaining
the originals. The graph is acyclic and no pair appears in both directions,
so 48 is the exact resulting dynasty count. `(jc1,j)` is then present;
`(jc1,c)` remains absent. Rule deletion empties dynasty because it has no own
assertions or remaining producing rules in that independent scenario.

The existing graph-traversal function is an independent oracle for all these
pair sets. Counts alone are insufficient. Compare exact pairs and added/removed
pairs against traversal, then optionally compare with fresh recomputation.
Internal domain facts, generated rule predicates, and engine-specific assertion
wrappers must not be included in the cross-engine contract.

Four original family questions have ordinary positive-ground or answer-set
contracts directly available to all engines: Johannes's descendants,
Ambrosius's descendants, Johannes's ancestry of Wilhelm Friedemann, and the
cross-branch dynasty membership test. The remaining three questions ask whether
the ontology entails universal symmetry/transitivity statements. Current pairs
cannot answer those questions: the original dynasty relation happens to equal
the transitive ancestor relation, although dynasty transitivity is not entailed.
Exclude these schema-service questions from the shared pair benchmark, or
define and separately document sound fresh-instance probes for every system.
Never substitute an empirical finite-relation test.

## Measurement and baseline rules

- Preserve the source tree at `b1254c4`; it predates the Bach harness. Run that
  package from an isolated source snapshot using the new external harness.
  These will be *new observations of the preserved baseline*, not Bach results
  that existed in its original saved report. Do not modify the saved baseline.
- Use the same input bytes for the OWL arms and document the exact, semantics-
  preserving translation to the two-rule family program for ZodiacEdge. All
  converters must operate on assertions and axioms, not an already inferred
  graph. Record their time or clearly exclude it symmetrically from the
  reasoning comparison; keep a full input-to-answer measurement as well.
- HermiT may defer inference until consistency or answer requests. Therefore
  compare a task interval that includes loading/preparation, consistency when
  required by the track, and fully consumed answers. Do not compare one
  reasoner's construction time with another's completed inference time.
- Record internal phase timings only as explanatory measurements. Define a
  complete task timer around the public operations, since sums of internal
  counters may omit graph copies and other preparation.
- For family changes, count the entire change application and retrieval of the
  two updated pair sets. Include a required `flush`, reclassification, or
  fallback reconstruction. Report whether the engine retained state or rebuilt;
  reconstruction is a valid method, but not evidence of incremental maintenance.
- Apply the fact removal and insertion before exposing the changed answers.
  If an interface has no atomic transaction, document its sequence and include
  every intermediate recomputation it requires. Do not time only the cheaper
  half. Buffering may permit one synchronization after both ontology changes.
- Keep original-state setup outside the update interval but report it
  separately. Do not let validation or a pre-query perform an update's deferred
  inference before the timer. Exact independent checks occur after the timed
  answers are obtained.
- Separate complete-task time from fresh-process wall time, which includes
  runtime startup, serialization and shutdown. The original examples are tiny;
  absolute milliseconds and startup costs matter alongside ratios.
- Use repeated blocks with rotating engine order, one benchmark worker at a
  time, and retain every observation. Fresh processes do not imply cold disk
  caches. Pair ratios within blocks, not ratios of independently chosen minima.
  Document sample count and use descriptive ranges for small samples.
- Timeouts, unsupported questions, incomplete reasoning, and wrong answers are
  different outcomes. None is a successful zero-time observation. A failed
  answer check disqualifies that sample from a valid speed comparison while
  remaining visible in the report.

## Optional scaling and additional controls

First establish exact results on the unmodified source examples. Deterministic
replication of the family graph can then be labelled a **derived synthetic
scaling experiment**, not additional historical Bach genealogy. Rename all
individuals in each copy; retain the same two relationship predicates and
rules. No edge may connect copies. With `k` copies, all four output-count rows
above multiply by `k` when the designated update is applied to every copy.
Depth and branching per copy remain fixed, so this measures replication scale,
not increasingly deep ancestry.

A separate one-copy update amid many unaffected copies can test the effect of
unchanged surrounding data. State this different change fraction explicitly.
Do not pool it with updates applied to all copies.

Scaling the full ontology requires more care because `leipzig` occurs in a
named-value restriction. Renaming that individual only in observations would
change the intended inferences. Sharing it across copies would not produce
fully disjoint copies. If this track is scaled, specify the treatment of
ontology constants and provide a separately checked expected-answer generator.

Useful correctness controls are already represented by the manifest and tests:
the positive Ambrosius father answer together with the negative named-child
answer; empty wives and mothers; surviving Johannes ancestry; removal of all
dynasty consequences after rule deletion; and the corrected negative
cross-branch symmetry example. A deliberately inconsistent full-ontology
control can also use the existing test equating `Voice` with `owl:Thing`, which
conflicts with the required non-voice instrument. Such a modified control is
an explicitly constructed extension, and no ordinary answers should be compared
after inconsistency is established.
